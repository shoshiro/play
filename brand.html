IThe name

A trace left on a white page.

Veshiro joins a Latin root and a Japanese one. It sounds like nothing else, spells the same in every language, and fits every platform handle at seven letters.

Latin vestigium A footprint, a trace. The mark that proves someone was here.
Japanese shiro 白 · 城 White, the blank page. Also a castle: a house that holds things of value.
Together ve · shiro Your mark on the blank page, kept inside a house. And a quiet nod to where it began, shoshiro.
IIThe seal

The name, written as a single line.

  1. The keyed wheel. The 26 letters sit around a circle, but not in order. Each letter moves eleven steps from the last, which shuffles the alphabet into the house's own sequence.
  2. The path. One stroke visits V, E, S, H, I, R and O in turn, bowing toward the centre between letters. The shape it makes, a gate under a crown, belongs to this name alone.
  3. The terminals. The line opens with a small ring and closes with a bar, the way hand-cut sigils always have. Letters in play are marked in gilt on the tick ring.
  4. The house order.

VIIVoice

Say less. Name the material, the number, the date.

  • No exclamation marks, no emoji, no countdowns. Scarcity is stated, never shouted.
  • Numbers are exact: Nº 004, forty made, ships 3 October.
  • Things are "pieces" and "editions", never "content", "drops" or "merch".
  • Prices are shown plainly. The house does not run sales.
  • Write like a letter to one person, not an announcement to a crowd.
